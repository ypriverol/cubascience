# How many people has Cuba lost since 2019? An interval estimate and a projection to end-2026

*A rigorous attempt to measure — and forecast — the largest demographic collapse in the Western Hemisphere in peacetime.*

**Status:** English draft outline aligned with `../demographics.ipynb`. Full prose is expanded in the notebook; this file is the citeable manuscript spine.

**Author:** Yasset Pérez-Riverol (2026)

## Abstract

We estimate Cuba’s population decline from end-2019 through end-2025 using the demographic accounting identity and Monte Carlo simulation (four models), triangulated against independent administrative registers. Preferred Model D (infant-mortality sentinel) places the end-2025 population near **8.56 million** (≈ **−2.3 million**, **−21%** vs 2019). Emigration dominates (~86% of the loss); age-standardized excess mortality in 2024–2025 is ≈40–60 thousand. We project further decline through end-2026.

**Keywords:** Cuba; depopulation; emigration; excess mortality; Monte Carlo; ONEI

## 1. Introduction

Three incompatible population series (UN, ONEI, independent estimates) frame the problem. No census since 2012. Motivation and adversarial review framing as in the notebook.

## 2. Three versions of the truth

UN (~10.9 M) · ONEI (9.43 M end-2025) · Albizu-Campos (~8.0–8.6 M). Honest end-2024 range roughly 8–11 M.

## 3. What caused the fall

Emigration dominant; registered deaths largely complete but elevated; births at a 125-year low (TFR 1.29).

## 4–7. Models A–D

Conservative ONEI-anchored (A); crisis-adjusted (B); analog-calibrated worst case (C); **sentinel-anchored Model D (preferred)**.

## 8. Triangulation

Eight independent sources; ceilings vs occupied-housing / independent estimates; band **8.0–8.9 M**.

## 9. Excess mortality

Age-standardized counterfactual (Kitagawa-style); ≈40–60k excess deaths in 2024–2025.

## 10. Selectivity and destinations

Working-age concentration; destinations ledger (settled only).

## 11. Projection to end-2026

Shared directional decline across models; Model D ≈8.27 M end-2026 (see notebook for intervals).

## 12. Discussion and limitations

Migration registration lag; delayed census; structural uncertainty across models ≠ within-model CI.

## 13. Conclusions

Dual engine: selective emigration plus internal demographic collapse. Official headcount understates the living population shortfall.
