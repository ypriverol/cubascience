\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.}
Cuba is losing population at one of the fastest peacetime rates in the Western Hemisphere.
No census has been completed since 2012, and the national statistics office (ONEI) removed more than one million people from its published headcount in a single 2024 revision.
End-2025 published levels still span roughly 8.0--11.0~million.
This paper audits the 2021--2025 headcount decline with transparent scenario ensembles, decomposes migration versus internal demographic change, and reports an end-2026 scenario continuation.

\textbf{Methods.}
We solve the demographic accounting identity with Monte Carlo simulation ($N=10^6$) under prior sets labeled Models~A--D (scenarios, not identified estimators), with optional Gaussian-copula dependence among baseline overstatement, migration, and death registration.
Model~D is an \emph{illustrative central scenario}: migration priors comparable to Model~B, with residual death under-registration tightened after an infant-mortality consistency check (a Venezuela analogy, not structural identification).
External registers are used as ceilings or soft corroboration; Model~D is never treated as external validation of itself.
Crude registered excess mortality is reported separately from a \emph{provisional} 2019-schedule residual (pending a full ONEI age--sex pipeline).

\textbf{Results.}
Across scenarios, cumulative 2021--2025 loss spans roughly \textbf{1.8--2.4~million} (the primary uncertainty statement).
Under the illustrative central scenario (Model~D), living population is near \textbf{8.59~million} at end-2025 (median loss \textbf{2.21~million}, \textbf{20.5\%} on a corrected 2021 base under fixed priors; within-scenario 90\% band for loss roughly 1.84--2.60~million).
Under those same Model~D priors, emigration accounts for about \textbf{91\%} of absolute loss.
A provisional vital-reconstruction companion (Model~E) yields $\sim$8.96~million and remains non-preferred.
An end-2026 scenario continuation under Model~D sits near \textbf{8.29~million}.

\textbf{Conclusions.}
Depopulation is predominantly migratory in the headcount, reinforced by collapsing births, elevated mortality, and accelerated ageing.
Against the \emph{official} 2021 stock, the end-2026 register gap approaches one in four (a different definition from Model~D's $-$20.5\% on the corrected base).
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Cuba; demography; emigration; natural decrease; infant mortality; fertility; ageing; statistical data quality.

\section{Introduction}

Counting Cubans has become contested.
The last completed census was in 2012; the census planned for 2022 has been repeatedly postponed and is now promised for 2026~\citep{onei_census}.
Without a census, published population depends on projections and a lagging migration register~\citep{preston2001,un_wpp}.
In July 2024, ONEI reported an ``effective population'' of 10{,}055{,}968 at end-2023 (about one million below prior published levels), then 9{,}748{,}007 (end-2024) and 9{,}434{,}593 (end-2025)~\citep{onei_revision,onei_2025}.

Three incompatible series coexist (Figure~\ref{fig:pop}):
the United Nations World Population Prospects path near 10.9~million (still assuming net emigration of order $2\times10^4$ per year)~\citep{un_wpp,eclac2022};
ONEI's revised official series (9.43~million at end-2025);
and independent studies placing the living population nearer 8.0--8.6~million~\citep{albizu2023,albizu2024}.
Almost three million people separate the extremes.
This paper presents a scenario audit of the 2021--2025 headcount decline with transparent prior ensembles, decomposes migratory and internal components, compares official migration accounting with destination registers, and reports an end-2026 scenario continuation.

Cuba's fertility transition was already among Latin America's earliest and deepest~\citep{diazbriquets1982,hollerbach1984,diazbriquets2014}, so below-replacement fertility is not new.
What is new is the \emph{speed} of headcount loss under combined emigration and natural decrease, in a regional context where Latin America and the Caribbean still grow overall~\citep{eclac2022,un_wpp}.
Comparable peacetime contractions elsewhere have been driven primarily by emigration (e.g.\ Venezuela)~\citep{coleman2006,pagegarcia2020}.

\textbf{Central thesis.}
The headcount loss is mostly emigration, but a second ``internal'' engine (birth decline, excess mortality, and ageing) would sustain contraction even if emigration slowed~\citep{lee2014,lesthaeghe2010}.
The two engines are not independent: selective exit of working-age adults and women of reproductive age causes much of the fertility decline, so many ``births that do not occur'' are a delayed migratory effect~\citep{bongaarts2009,diazbriquets2014}.

\section{Methods}

The balancing equation is the standard demographic accounting identity~\citep{preston2001}.
Registered ONEI aggregates over 2022--2025 are fixed anchors~\citep{onei_2025}:
births $B^{\mathrm{reg}}=325{,}233$, deaths $D^{\mathrm{reg}}=502{,}149$,
and identity-consistent net emigration $R=1{,}501{,}706$.
Official end-2021 population is $P^{\mathrm{off}}_{2021}=11{,}113{,}215$.
Each Monte Carlo draw samples latent corrections and applies
\begin{align}
P_{2021} &= P^{\mathrm{off}}_{2021} - U_0, \\
D &= D^{\mathrm{reg}}\,D_{\mathrm{fac}} \;\;(+\;\text{optional epidemic pulse in B/C}), \\
M_{\mathrm{net}} &= R\,M, \\
\Delta &= (D - B) + M_{\mathrm{net}}, \\
P_{2025} &= P_{2021} - \Delta,
\end{align}
where $U_0$ is end-2021 baseline overstatement (people already effectively abroad but still on the roll),
$M\ge 1$ scales ONEI net emigration, and $D_{\mathrm{fac}}\ge 1$ scales registered deaths.
Percent loss uses the \emph{corrected} base $P_{2021}$, not $P^{\mathrm{off}}_{2021}$.
We draw $N=10^6$ scenarios.
In Model~D, $(U_0,M,D_{\mathrm{fac}})$ may be coupled by a Gaussian copula on latent normals; the numeric $\rho$ matrix is an illustrative dependence assumption (default scientific reading treats medians as driven by margins; an independence sensitivity barely moves them).
Within-scenario 90\% bands are \emph{prior-predictive} spreads under fixed Beta margins---not sampling error from data.
The \textbf{range across Models~A--D} ($\sim$1.8--2.4~million of cumulative loss) is the primary structural uncertainty statement~\citep{repo,modeld}.

We define five prior sets.
\textbf{A (conservative)} uses modest $U_0$ and $M$ near ONEI with a small death multiplier (\texttt{model\_from\_2021.py}).
\textbf{B (crisis-adjusted)} raises $M$, allows death under-registration up to $\sim$14\%, and adds an explicit epidemic pulse.
\textbf{C (upper stress)} pushes $U_0$/$M$ and under-registration further (cap $\sim$18\%) with a large elderly mortality pulse.
\textbf{D (illustrative central)} keeps migration priors comparable to B but \emph{tightens} death under-registration after the infant-mortality consistency check (Table~\ref{tab:priors}).
\textbf{E (provisional companion)} is a vital reconstruction via a health-system deterioration score (supplement) and is not preferred for public claims.
Models~A--D are \textbf{scenarios} (prior narratives), not scientifically identified estimators of a unique living population.
Model~D is preferred as an illustrative central path inside the across-scenario envelope because its migration prior matches B while replacing B's wide mortality pulse with a sentinel-bounded $D_{\mathrm{fac}}$---not because $M$ is uniquely identified by a likelihood.
Models~B and~D yield nearly identical headcount medians because migration priors dominate absolute loss.
ONEI's 2023--2024 revisions already absorbed a large lagged stock; scaling revised $R$ by $M>1$ \emph{and} subtracting large $U_0$ can double-count the same outdated-register defect.
We therefore always report the ONEI-anchored neighbourhood (Model~A / $M\approx 1$) beside Model~D, and treat $U_0+(M-1)R$ as constrained by destination floors plus documented lag---not as unbounded free parameters.

Infant mortality rose from 5.0 to 9.9 per thousand live births (2019--2025), a $\sim$98\% increase.
Infant deaths are comparatively hard to hide (hospital occurrence; international scrutiny), so IMR is treated as a high-integrity sentinel of health-system \emph{stress}, not as a calibrator of elderly death completeness.
As a loose foreign analogy (not a Cuban structural parameter), Venezuela's crisis saw IMR rise $\sim$76\% (roughly 2012--2017) alongside an all-cause crude death-rate rise of order $\sim$25\%~\citep{pagegarcia2020,garcia2019,venezuela_imr}.
A log-elasticity reading is
\begin{equation}
e=\frac{\ln(1.25)}{\ln(1.76)}\approx 0.39,
\end{equation}
so Cuba's IMR rise would predict $(1.98)^{0.39}-1\approx 31\%$ higher CDR if the analogy transferred.
Registered Cuban CDR rose from 9.7 to 12.9 per thousand by 2024 ($\sim$33\%), roughly consistent with that order of magnitude~\citep{onei_2025}.
This remains a \textbf{consistency check against very large hidden institutional undercounts}, not identification of $D_{\mathrm{fac}}$; residual under-registration in Model~D is still limited to a small at-home elderly channel (central $\sim$3\%, cap 9\%; Table~\ref{tab:priors})~\citep{modeld}.

Two distinct mortality quantities are reported and must not be added or substituted~\citep{karlinsky2021,msemburi2023,kishore2018}.
For crude registered excess, on crisis-adjusted mid-year stocks $\tilde P_t$, we define a no-crisis counterfactual CDR with a mild ageing drift from the 2019 level 9.7 per thousand,
$\mathrm{CDR}^{\mathrm{cf}}_t=9.7+0.16(t-2019)$, and
\begin{equation}
E^{\mathrm{crude}}_t = D^{\mathrm{reg}}_t - \mathrm{CDR}^{\mathrm{cf}}_t\cdot \tilde P_t/1000.
\end{equation}
Summing 2020--2025 yields $\sum E^{\mathrm{crude}}_t\approx 153{,}000$ (of which $\sim$66{,}000 in 2024--2025); most of this toll is already inside registered death totals, and the linear CDR drift is a transparent baseline, not full age standardization.
Separately, Figure~\ref{fig:excess} compares registered deaths to \emph{illustrative} expected deaths under a provisional 2019 schedule bridge ($\sim$95k and $\sim$92k versus registered 128.1k and 136.2k), implying a combined residual of order $\sim$77{,}000 for 2024--2025 (\texttt{scripts/mortality\_schedule\_residual.py}).
A wider 65{,}000--90{,}000 band is retained as sensitivity to population-stock choice pending a full ONEI table 3.15 age--sex application to explicit $P_{a,t}$~\citep{preston2001}.
We deliberately avoid calling this a Kitagawa decomposition: Kitagawa separates rate versus composition contributions to a rate difference; here we report a direct expected-deaths residual~\citep{kitagawa1955}.
Mortality rates at ages 60+ remaining 11--27\% above 2019 are consistent with elevated age-specific risks, but that claim also awaits full schedule publication.

Published population levels are grouped by information content (Figure~\ref{fig:tri}).
Ceilings that do not purge emigrants include the UN World Population Prospects, the MINSAP denominator, and the electoral roll (soft upper bounds only).
The official ONEI revised headcount is 9.43~M at end-2025.
Exit-correcting or independent constructs include housing$\times$occupancy ($\sim$8.7~M; fragile occupancy scalar) and Albizu-Campos 2023/2024 (8.0--8.6~M; methods that share migration-critique assumptions with this paper).
Model~D (8.59~M) is the study estimand and is \emph{not} external validation of itself; agreement with Albizu/housing is partly shared-prior echo, while ceilings bound the upside.
The soft band from exit-correcting sources is near 8.0--8.9~million.

The settled-concept destination floor (Table~\ref{tab:bridge}) comprises United States $\sim$800{,}000 (administrative/CBP-pipeline statuses, \emph{not} ACS stock), non-U.S.\ total $\sim$250{,}000 (Spain $\sim$135{,}000 + Uruguay $\sim$35{,}000 + rest $\sim$80{,}000), and floor $\sim$1.05~million~\citep{destinations,pew_cuban}.
ACS Cuban-born stock rose by only $\sim$335{,}000 from 2019--2024---a lower stock-change floor that must not be silently equated with the 800{,}000 administrative figure.
Encounter counts and nationality applications are separate concepts and are never added into the settled floor~\citep{cbp_encounters}.

Destination data do \emph{not} identify Model~D's $\sim$2.0~million net emigration; they support a large floor and leave the 1.05$\to$2.0 path as soft gap-filling plus a migration prior.
If true net emigration were nearer 1.5 than 2.0~million, living population would sit toward $\sim$8.9 rather than $\sim$8.6~million; cumulative loss would remain large and emigration would still dominate absolute loss under every scenario in Table~\ref{tab:models}.
Conditional on end-2025 draws, the end-2026 scenario continuation~\label{sec:nowcast} uses births centered at 65{,}682 ($\mathcal{N}$ noise 3\%),
deaths centered at $1.04\times 136{,}214$ times the same $D_{\mathrm{fac}}$,
and a three-regime net emigration mixture with probabilities $(0.35,0.35,0.30)$ on centers $(150{,}000,\;210{,}000,\;300{,}000)$ plus 15\% log-scale noise.
The $\sim$4\% death lift and regime weights are scenario assumptions, not a validated forecast.
Paths to $\sim$7~million by 2030 in Figure~\ref{fig:pop} are \textbf{illustrative trend scenarios} (visually the post-2026 segment); they are not probabilistic forecasts.

\section{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:models} summarizes end-2025 medians for Models~A--E.
The scientifically primary uncertainty statement is the across-scenario loss envelope ($\sim$1.8--2.4~million).
Illustrative Model~D yields median population \textbf{8.59~million} and loss \textbf{2.21~million} (20.5\% on the corrected base under fixed priors; within-scenario 90\% band 1.84--2.60~million).
Under Model~D priors, migration's share of absolute loss is about \textbf{91\%}.
Model~A (ONEI-anchored, $M\approx 1$) remains the conservative floor neighbourhood ($\sim$9.16~M).
Provisional Model~E yields $\sim$8.96~M (loss 2.13~M): same loss band, higher stock, partly because its $U_0$ prior is much smaller than Model~D's; an adversarial gate does not promote E over D~\citep{modele_supp}.
Percentage language: against official 2021 (11.11~M), end-2025 register-gap loss is roughly 20--23\%; against the corrected study base it is about 16--23\% across A--D.
A 2026 scenario continuation places Model~D near \textbf{8.29~million} (further loss $\sim$300{,}000; across-scenario band roughly 7.9--8.9~million).

Table~\ref{tab:bridge} separates settled counts from encounters, ACS stock change, and nationality applications.
The settled floor ($\sim$1.05~M) confirms that emigration is large while remaining a lower bound.
Closing the gap to ONEI's $\sim$1.5~million is an unquantified residual; Model~D's $\sim$2.0~million is a prior on $R$, not destination identification.
If true net is nearer 1.5 than 2.0~million, living population sits toward the high end of our band ($\sim$8.9 rather than 8.6~M).
Emigration still dominates absolute loss under every scenario in Table~\ref{tab:models}.

Reducing the crisis to emigration alone is incomplete: emigration can reverse, but missing births and an aged residual population cannot.
A useful distinction is \textbf{realized loss} (exit-dominated) versus \textbf{future inertia} (natural decrease increasingly important).
In 2019 the natural balance was still slightly positive ($+$636); in 2025 deaths exceeded births by \textbf{68{,}150} (68{,}064 births versus 136{,}214 deaths; Figure~\ref{fig:scissors}).
That 2025 deficit matches the 2021 COVID peak without a pandemic: natural decrease is now structural.
The total fertility rate fell to 1.29 children per woman in 2025, the lowest since 1958 and well below replacement (2.1)~\citep{diazbriquets2014,bongaarts2009,lesthaeghe2010}.
Emigration removes young women (majority-female flow) and those who remain defer births under scarcity.
Births fell 38\% (from $\sim$110{,}000 in 2019 to 68{,}000 in 2025).
A back-of-envelope split into ``half fewer women / half lower TFR'' is \emph{illustrative only}: ONEI TFR denominators may themselves be inflated by absent women, which would overstate the rate half; a formal exposure--ASFR decomposition is not claimed here.
Official projections move the share aged 60+ from 20.4\% (2019) to 26.7\% (2025) and toward 33\% by 2035 under a larger living stock than Model~D~\citep{onei_2025,lee2014}.
Under a smaller, youth-depleted stock the elderly share would be higher; any $\sim$29\% / $\sim$40\% figures in companion graphics are \textbf{illustrative sketches}, not cohort-component estimates.
Median age reached \textbf{45} years in 2025 on official reporting (Figure~\ref{fig:aging}).

Official life expectancy at birth peaked in 2011--13 (78.5 years) and fell to 77.7 in 2018--20; an independent 2021 estimate places it near 71 years~\citep{albizu2023,bronnum2023}.
That decline is large relative to COVID-era international losses~\citep{aburto2022,msemburi2023}; LE re-expresses mortality rather than independently corroborating Model~D (series in the supplement).
Infant deaths rose from 461 (2018) to 715 (2022); the rate reached 9.9 per thousand in 2025 ($+$98\% since 2019).
Figure~\ref{fig:excess} reports a \emph{provisional} 2019-schedule residual of order $\sim$77{,}000 for 2024--2025 combined (sensitivity band 65{,}000--90{,}000; coarse bridge in \texttt{scripts/mortality\_schedule\_residual.py}), distinct from crude registered excess of $\sim$153{,}000 over 2020--2025~\citep{karlinsky2021}.

About \textbf{77\%} of emigrants are aged 15--59; nearly half are under 35 (Figure~\ref{fig:age}).
Among those remaining, official structure places about 26.7\% at ages 60+ (median age $\sim$45)---not a Model~D-consistent stock.
Exit hollows the centre of the pyramid and removes those who would have children~\citep{coleman2006,lee2014}.
The flow is majority female~\citep{albizu2023} and concentrated at working ages (Figure~\ref{fig:age}).
Subnational outflow is uneven: ONEI-reported Havana net emigration is about \textbf{$-$33 per 1{,}000} in 2025 (provincial ranks more reliable than levels if national migration accounting is biased; map in the supplement).
Together, mortality and selectivity close the internal story of who dies, who leaves, and who remains (Figure~\ref{fig:mortality_selectivity}).

\section{Discussion}

Model~D is retained as the illustrative central scenario because the IMR consistency check shrinks the need for large hidden-death multipliers and focuses residual uncertainty on migration~\citep{pagegarcia2020,modeld}.
It is not an identified point estimate: absolute loss remains $\sim$91\% migratory under D's priors, and $U_0$ with $M>1$ can share a common outdated-register defect.
Provisional Model~E lands in the same loss band with a higher stock ($\sim$8.96~M) and is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:models} and the supplement~\citep{modele_supp}; its adversarial gate (different $U_0$, ageing double-count risk in $S_t$, migration still identifying $P^*$) keeps E non-preferred.
Within-scenario intervals look falsely precise relative to the across-scenario envelope---the envelope should lead.
The scientific lead is Model~D $\sim$20.5\% loss by end-2025 on the \emph{corrected} base (about one in five); as a register-gap aside, against official 2021 (11.11~M), cumulative gap by end-2026 approaches $\sim$25\% (about one in four).
These are not interchangeable headlines.
Exit is age- and sex-selective; it ages the island and structurally depresses fertility~\citep{coleman2006,diazbriquets2014,lee2014}.
The natural balance, positive in 2019, fell to $-$68{,}150 in 2025; the provisional schedule residual in 2024--2025 is consistent with elevated mortality beyond ageing alone, pending full age--sex computation~\citep{karlinsky2021,msemburi2023}.
WFP food-insecurity and anaemia indicators, and the fall in agricultural GDP, are discussed only as \emph{contextual} amplifying pathways~\citep{wfp_cuba}; they do not enter $D_{\mathrm{fac}}$ or excess formulas and are not identified causes of the death or birth series.
ONEI's declared 2022 net migration ($+$991) is inconsistent with hundreds of thousands of U.S.\ Cuban arrivals that year at any plausible encounter-to-emigrant conversion (encounters are not unique net emigrants 1:1).
The accounting relocates much of the exodus into a single 2023 revision jump.
Even after that revision, 2024--2025 net balances remain only slightly above U.S.-only recorded entries, while the 2025 balance admits 317{,}320 gross external emigrations (net $-$245{,}264): continued understatement of exit.
This paper therefore treats the official headcount as a ceiling and contrasts it with exit-correcting sources (Figure~\ref{fig:tri}).

\section{Limitations}
No post-2012 census; ONEI's $\sim$24-month emigration rule lags recent exits; destination microdata are incomplete; U.S.\ ``settled'' administrative totals are not ACS stock; the IMR analogy is not a Cuban structural parameter; housing-occupancy assumptions are fragile; ageing sketches are not cohort-component projections; life expectancy re-expresses mortality; 2026--2030 paths are scenarios.
$U_0$ and $M$ can double-count register lag; destination encounter ceilings can inflate unique persons; within-scenario intervals give false precision relative to structural uncertainty.
The provisional 2019-schedule residual is not yet a published age--sex computation from ONEI 3.15.
Model~E's higher population partly reflects a different $U_0$ prior.
Food-insecurity indicators are contextual only.

\section{Conclusions}
Across scenarios, Cuba's 2021--2025 population loss lies near 1.8--2.4~million (primary statement).
Under illustrative Model~D priors, living population is near 8.59~million at end-2025 (not the official 9.43~million), with median loss 2.21~million (20.5\% on the corrected base) and emigration about 91\% of absolute loss; Model~A remains the ONEI-anchored floor neighbourhood.
By end-2026 the Model~D scenario continuation is near 8.29~million; the register gap against the official 2021 base approaches one in four---a different definition from $-$20.5\%.
A provisional schedule residual of order $\sim$77{,}000 deaths in 2024--2025 (sensitivity 65--90k) is secondary to the migration scenario envelope and must be kept distinct from crude registered excess of $\sim$153{,}000 over 2020--2025.
A 2026 census conducted under standard practice would be informative; the working scenario band is 8.6--9.0~million rather than the 10.9~million World Population Prospects path under still-low net-emigration assumptions~\citep{un_wpp}.
